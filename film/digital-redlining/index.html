About the film

Redlining was the practice of marking neighborhoods as bad risks for lending, and the lines it drew did lasting damage to who could build wealth and where. The film starts there and asks a plain question: where does that history go now?

Part of the answer is data. Algorithms learn from records, and records carry the choices that produced them. A system trained on a world shaped by exclusion can reproduce that exclusion without anyone intending it, in housing, lending, healthcare, and policing. The film follows that thread from the historical research to the systems in use today, and also looks at how the same tools can be used to find and reduce these gaps.

I built it from my own research into redlining and wealth disparities, and from two interviews: one with a historian of housing segregation and one with a computer scientist who studies algorithmic fairness. One speaks to the history, the other to the systems built on top of it. I made the film in Final Cut Pro and Motion.

Why it's here

My day-to-day work is judging whether AI output is good. This film asks the question underneath that one: good according to whom, and based on what history. I made it before the Mercor and Egnyte work.
